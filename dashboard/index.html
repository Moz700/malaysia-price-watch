Five findings

The findings cover a year of prices from every state. Snapshot comparisons use September 2026, and each shop counts once.

1 Shop format

Hypermarkets were 9.0% cheaper than sundry shops on the same 181 items

  • Hypermarkets were the cheapest format for 159 of 242 items compared; sundry shops were the dearest for 100.
  • Hypermarkets priced 20.0% of their items above the national median; sundry shops priced 67.9% above it.
  • The cheapest chains with 5 or more branches were Tesco and Lotus's (89.3 to 89.4) and Econsave (90.2).

Who should act: retail category managers, by benchmarking prices against hypermarkets and discount chains.

On the same 181 items, the price index runs from 95.0 at hypermarkets to 104.3 at sundry shops

Price index by shop type, September 2026. 100 = national median price of the same items.

Source: q03, q30 (PriceCatcher, KPDN)

2 Fresh produce

Fresh produce cost more at wet markets than at hypermarkets and supermarkets

  • Half of all wet markets (49.5%) sit in their state's dearest quarter of shops, against 8.7% of hypermarkets.
  • Standard chicken cost a median RM 8.99 per kg at hypermarkets and RM 10.50 at wet markets in September 2026.
  • A hypermarket or supermarket beat the wet market on fresh produce in 14 of 16 states.

Who should act: KPDN, by pointing out the price gap between shop types in its consumer price-comparison messages.

On fresh produce, wet markets averaged 106.5 against 97.7 at hypermarkets

Average shop's fresh-produce price index by shop type, September 2026. 100 = national median.

Source: q12; quarter shares from premise_index.csv (q19)

3 Location

The same basket costs 9.9% more in Kuala Lumpur than in Kedah

  • The five dearest are the federal territories and East Malaysia: Kuala Lumpur, Sarawak, Putrajaya, Labuan and Sabah.
  • Inside Kuala Lumpur, district basket indexes run from 100.4 (Bandar Tun Razak) to 112.0 (Segambut).
  • 30 districts had only one to three reporting shops, 22 of them in Sabah or Sarawak, and 12 listed districts had none.

Who should act: KPDN, by adding reporting shops in sparsely covered Sabah and Sarawak districts and by enforcing at district level.

4 Price changes

Most prices held steady, and a few fresh-food categories drove the increases

  • 242 of 288 items (84%), tracked in the same shops, moved by less than 5% between October 2025 and September 2026.
  • The biggest riser was tomatoes, +69.4%, with 95% of 1,518 shops raising the price.
  • Controlled staples did not move: coarse sugar stayed at RM 2.85 and packet cooking oil at RM 2.50.

Who should act: procurement teams, by using fixed-price contracts for packaged goods and flexible ones for vegetables, onions and meat.

Vegetables, onions and garlic, and meat made up 58% of all price increases with 17% of the items

Source: q14, q13

5 Timing

Festive staples got cheaper before Chinese New Year and Hari Raya, and spike weeks peaked in December 2025

  • In the 14 days before Chinese New Year and Hari Raya Aidilfitri, festive staples got cheaper, and by more than other items.
  • Weekly price spikes were less common in pre-festival weeks (2.83%) than in other weeks (4.52%).
  • December 2025 had 78 spike weeks, 74 of them fresh produce, more than any other month.

Who should act: KPDN, by watching fresh vegetables in December as closely as in festive periods; procurement, by securing fresh produce before December.

    Period

    October 2025 to September 2026. The state, district, shop and item price views use September 2026. The monthly, weekly and festive views cover the whole year.

    A caveat on inflation

    data.gov.my describes PriceCatcher as a price surveillance dataset and names DOSM's CPI as the official inflation measure. This project uses CPI only as a consistency check (q27).

    Item names

    Item names stay as KPDN publishes them, in Malay, so they match the source files.

    The map

    State outlines come from DOSM's administrative_1_state boundaries, simplified to load quickly. East Malaysia is drawn closer to the peninsula than it really is, so both fit the page. Kuala Lumpur, Putrajaya and Labuan are too small to click on the outline, so each also has a labelled marker.

    How the price index works

    Each shop's price for each item is divided by that item's national median price in September 2026. The shop's price index is the average of those ratios times 100. 100 means the shop prices like the national median; below 100 means cheaper.

    How the basket index works

    The basket holds 265 items, each priced in at least 13 of the 16 states. A state's basket cost is the sum of its median price for each item; where a state has no price, the national median fills in. Basket index = state basket cost / national basket cost x 100.

    A district's index is the average ratio of its median price to the national median over the basket items it prices. Districts with too few items priced are shown in grey and not scored.

    Two different shop-type numbers

    The heatmap and the shop-type charts show the average shop's own price index (q12): hypermarket 98.4, supermarket 101.4, mini market 103.4, sundry shop 106.8, wet market 108.2. Finding 1 instead compares the same 181 items across all five formats (q03): hypermarket 95.0 to sundry shop 104.3. Both use 100 = national median, but they answer different questions, so the numbers differ.

    Data quality

    Seven checks: missing or impossible prices (76 rows), exact duplicates (0), two prices for one shop, item and day (0), item codes not in the item list (313,598), premise codes not in the premise list (13), extreme outliers (3,115), and shops that start late or stop early (flagged, not removed). In total 316,802 rows (1.51%) were removed.
